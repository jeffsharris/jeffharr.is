Dharmette: Dharma in interpersonal life 1 of 5

AudioDharma 15:33 2026-08-31

What happens to Dharma practice when we leave the cushion and enter the messy mirror of other people? The talk explores how relationships reveal intention versus impact, surface both kindness and reactive habit, and make clear that some wounds and some healings are only available in connection. It ends with a simple assignment: notice what changes in the mind, the self-story, and the felt life of Dharma when you are with others versus alone.

Chapters

  1. 00:00 From self to Dharma-centered - The talk opens by shifting from an egocentric life toward a Dharma-centered one aimed at reducing suffering and increasing freedom.
  2. 01:17 Practice needs others - The speaker argues that life is relational and that sangha matters because some help, insight, and wounds only arise with other people.
  3. 02:56 The mirror of relationship - Others function as a mirror for self-understanding, making the gap between intention and impact a place for honest investigation.
  4. 04:59 Humility and vulnerability - The interpersonal realm is described as a place of longing, shame, healing, and the unavoidable loss of ego’s preferred image.
  5. 06:43 Nervous system and virtue - A reference to Lisa Feldman Barrett underscores how other people can both regulate and destabilize the nervous system and intensify wholesome or unwholesome tendencies.
  6. 07:37 Personal turning points - Childhood and adolescent memories illustrate jealousy, family disillusionment, insecurity, anger, and the eventual arrival of a path.
  7. 10:21 Where practice is tested - The talk turns to how the Dharma generalizes imperfectly into relationships, where greed and hate can return with force and idealized self-images break down.
  8. 12:19 Courage, patience, ethics - The final movement frames interpersonal life as the field where insight stabilizes or fails, and where sila, samadhi, and panna are truly explored.
  9. 14:16 Homework for attention - The talk closes with a practice assignment to notice what others soothe or activate, and how companionship changes the story of self and the sense of Dharma.
