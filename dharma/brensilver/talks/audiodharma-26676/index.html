Exploring emotion with breathing as an anchor

AudioDharma 42:30 2026-11-18

What happens when breathing is not used to escape emotion, but as a steady place from which to feel it? The talk moves from the simple comfort of rain and embodied presence into a careful exploration of emotional currents, urgency, and the way mindfulness can make room for intensity without reacting to it. Again and again, the emphasis is on receiving experience directly: breathing, body, feeling, and the worlds they create.

Chapters

  1. 00:00 Rain and belonging - The talk opens with the rain and the felt sense of being held by nature, setting a tone of coziness and embeddedness.
  2. 02:39 Breathing as a check-in - Breath becomes a pulse check for the whole being, offering a simple way to sense body, attention, emotion, and energy.
  3. 06:09 Receiving the breath - The practice shifts toward resting with breathing as a small light or open room, allowing relaxation rather than effort.
  4. 08:34 Letting go of wanting - Attention is contrasted with wanting, and the emphasis turns to receiving the rhythms of in-breath and out-breath without grasping.
  5. 13:55 Making room for feeling - The body is imagined as a wide pasture, spacious enough to meet emotional currents and bodily activation without needing to define them.
  6. 18:02 Urgency meets permission - The talk explores urgency, the pressure to fix or acquire something, and the possibility of meeting all of it with radical permission.
  7. 22:23 Safety for emotional currents - Emotion is described as asking for responsiveness and care, while simple resting in breathing helps the inner life feel more secure.
  8. 28:20 Presence and disorientation - The talk stays with the disorientation of presence and absence, then widens to the idea that clinging is what names and fixes identity.
  9. 39:00 Feeling creates worlds - In closing, the talk returns to how pleasant and unpleasant feeling give rise to strategy, perception, and whole worlds, all as part of mindfulness of the body.
