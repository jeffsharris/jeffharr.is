Guided Meditation: Arriving in the Breath

AudioDharma 52:29 2026-07-27

What would it mean to leave yourself alone, and let the breath be enough? The talk begins with settling into the body, resting with something not urgent, and letting attention soften away from the usual pressure to manage experience. It then moves from breath awareness into forgiveness of the mind, and finally into practical walking meditation instructions that emphasize continuity, ease, and a pace that suits the body.

Chapters

  1. 00:00 Arriving and letting go - The talk opens by inviting a simple kind of arrival: sitting down, leaving oneself alone, and not immediately trying to shape experience.
  2. 02:29 Settling in the body - Attention widens to body sensations as a constellation, including the front and back of the body and the field in which breathing is noticed.
  3. 06:39 Breath as tether - The breath is presented as a steady place to rest, stay grounded in the present, and step out of the usual pull of opportunities and threats.
  4. 10:05 Resting with the breath - The guidance deepens into repeated resting with the breath at the nostrils, chest, or belly while keeping the mind relaxed.
  5. 14:49 Letting the world pass - The talk asks for permission for the rest of the world to arise and pass in the background, with a sustained rhythm of attention on the breath.
  6. 21:56 Forgiving the mind - The speaker encourages forgiving preoccupations, fear, and obsession, and bowing to concern rather than fighting it.
  7. 29:36 Suffering and ease - The talk reflects on craving, suffering, and the possibility of finding deeper safety by softening around experience and caring for what matters most.
  8. 40:41 Walking meditation basics - The second half turns to walking meditation as a practice equal in value to sitting, centered on continuity of attention and a manageable path length.
  9. 47:56 Rhythm, speed, and stance - The guidance closes with practical advice on internal attention, labeling steps, syncing breath with movement, and using a pace and posture that feel natural.
