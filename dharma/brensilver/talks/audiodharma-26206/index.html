Love, Grief and Dharma (Dharma and Interpersonal Life 4 of 5)

AudioDharma 15:14 2026-09-03

What if grief is not a failure of practice, but part of what makes love and Dharma real? The talk moves from impermanence and mortality into a defense of grief as spiritually fertile, arguing that a love untouched by loss is a naive love. It then widens into gratitude, tenderness, and the sense that living fully means allowing both poignancy and inconsolability without retreating into detachment.

Chapters

  1. 00:00 Opening the Question - The talk begins by framing the central link between love, finitude, and the meaning of life.
  2. 00:23 Love Needs Impermanence - Love is shown to matter because it ends, and the inevitability of loss is presented as woven into intimacy.
  3. 01:56 Why Grief Matters - The talk questions tidy spiritual language that places grief outside the path and argues for giving grief its due.
  4. 03:23 A Buddha Who Grieves - Understanding impermanence is distinguished from eliminating grief, and grief is defended as compatible with awakening.
  5. 04:50 Grief and Awakened Heart - A reference to Pema Chödrön is used to present grief as fertile, with love and art named as possible redemptions.
  6. 06:02 Growth Feels Like Loss - The talk turns to the way Dharma practice can itself feel like grieving as old assumptions about permanence fall away.
  7. 08:20 Self and Absence - Grief is described as part of loving relations deeply enough that the other becomes part of one’s self-model.
  8. 10:40 Death at the Center - A quotation from Naomi Shihab Nye leads into the idea that death is central to life and can make each day poignant.
  9. 12:53 Gratitude Without Denial - The closing movement asks for gratitude rooted in impermanence, while leaving room for wounds that cannot be fully consoled.
