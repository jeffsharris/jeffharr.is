Guided Meditation: Attention is the spear tip of karma

AudioDharma 26:46 2026-08-19

What happens when attention is treated as the spear tip of karma: something conditioned, not fully under will, yet still available as a gentle practice? The talk begins by loosening the grip of suffering through a simple turn toward breath, sound, body, and space, then shifts into a reflection on how we learn to stop fighting karma and meet what is here with less fear. It closes with a quiet emphasis on fidelity to the anchor of attention, letting go of self-engineering, and bowing to the lineage of those who have come before.

Chapters

  1. 00:12 Dharma Is Good - The talk opens with the felt sense that practice is good even when experience is difficult, because something larger than suffering is present.
  2. 00:56 Out of Gridlock - It points toward moving out of the claustrophobia of dukkha, clinging, and self-harshness into a more open space.
  3. 02:09 Gently Turning Attention - The practice becomes very simple: use the least effort needed to turn attention toward breath, sound, body, or the body-space boundary.
  4. 05:20 A Wider Space - The reminder returns that whatever suffering is here, something is always bigger than it, and attention can rest there.
  5. 08:09 Attention and Karma - Attention is described as the spear tip of karma, conditioned by many causes rather than governed only by willfulness.
  6. 09:09 Not Fighting Karma - The practice matures as a willingness to stop fighting karma, with the sense that the personal job is barely even there.
  7. 12:49 Loving What's Here - The closing reflection emphasizes learning to love what is here, fear a little less what is there, and trust the release of control.
