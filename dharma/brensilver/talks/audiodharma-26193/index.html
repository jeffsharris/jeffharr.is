Dharmette: Dharma in Our Interpersonal Life (2 of 5) Not Knowing and Intimacy

AudioDharma 15:02 2026-09-01

What happens when the stories we rely on to know ourselves and others become too fixed? The talk moves from the unease of asking “who am I?” into a practice of not knowing that makes room for intimacy, forgiveness, and a little more freedom in relationship. Along the way, it looks at the habits of mind that turn loved ones into roles, and it balances that with the need for some ordinary predictability in life.

Chapters

  1. 00:12 Who am I? - The talk opens with the question of selfhood and the sense that any answer feels slightly false or too small.
  2. 01:56 Mystery and space - Practice is presented as creating space around the self-story, allowing a more direct and less verbal relationship to experience.
  3. 03:54 Habit energies - The speaker describes subtle conditioning and latent tendencies that shape perception, especially under interpersonal pressure.
  4. 06:21 Meeting what is evoked - The focus turns to how relationships reveal our assumptions, and why practice asks us to loosen our grip on what we think we know.
  5. 07:36 Not knowing, forgiveness - A Zen line and Simone Weil are used to show how recognizing that self and others exceed our concepts can lead to forgiveness.
  6. 09:22 The need for safety - The talk explores the mind's drive to make love and life predictable, and how that can protect us while also narrowing connection.
  7. 11:43 Roles and flatness - Relationships become choreographed when people are frozen into roles, which can flatten intimacy and hide the other's fuller life.
  8. 14:10 Relaxing the grasp - The talk closes by inviting attention to these habits over the next day and a willingness to set down certainty with some wonder.
