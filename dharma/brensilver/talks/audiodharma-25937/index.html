Matthew Brensilver Dharma Talks

Guided meditation Anicca and Feelings

AudioDharma 40:42 2026-07-29

How do you meet feeling without immediately defending against it? The talk begins with forgiveness and compassion, then moves into a close investigation of mood, emotion, craving, and self-story as all being shaped by feeling. Again and again, the practice turns toward letting awareness saturate sensation, so that change can be known directly rather than managed from a distance.

Chapters

  1. 00:01 Compassion First - The meditation opens by softening toward oneself, the body, and the world through forgiveness and gratitude.
  2. 02:35 Opening Into Allowing - Forgiveness is linked to opening, and the talk begins shifting from protection toward simple allowing.
  3. 05:49 Feeling Shapes Perception - The speaker explores how all phenomena converge on feeling, how moods color perception, and how self-story is charged with affect.
  4. 13:23 Meeting Friction - Grasping and aversion around feeling are examined, along with the fear that feeling will threaten the inner self.
  5. 18:59 Steer Without Force - The practice is briefly framed as steering rather than controlling, with a reminder to stay present in the immediacy of now.
  6. 21:22 Craving and Impermanence - Craving is named as feeling-based and marked by dukkha, anicca, and anatta, shifting attention from promise to direct observation.
  7. 26:42 Let Awareness Saturate - The talk broadens to include discomfort, urge, stories, and even how the meditation is judged, all as feeling to be known calmly.
  8. 35:53 Prediction and Rebirth - The closing turns to the mind's habit of predicting itself, then returns to the central practice of gently caressing arousal, tension, and feeling.
