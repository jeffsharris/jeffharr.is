Dharmette: Flexibility

AudioDharma 14:55 2026-08-12

What happens when wisdom is less about being strong and more about being able to change shape? The talk argues that flexibility is central to practice: seeing impermanence, loosening clinging, and learning to meet each moment without defending a fixed self. It moves from the practical costs of rigidity in conflict and relationships to a dependent-arising view of self and world, ending with a quiet invitation to become useful rather than self-protective.

Chapters

  1. 00:10 Wisdom Depends - The talk opens by questioning fixed answers and suggesting that effective response to life depends on context and flexibility.
  2. 01:33 Strength as Fluidity - Impermanence and the trembling of things are framed as reasons that practice must be adaptable rather than rigid.
  3. 02:40 One Play Fails - Using a playful example, the speaker shows how repeating one strategy for every situation leads to trouble.
  4. 04:20 Clinging Narrows Us - Clinging, convention, and a narrow idea of happiness are described as the main forces that contract the heart and reduce freedom.
  5. 05:55 Self and Shame - The talk turns to ego, defensiveness, and shame, connecting inflexibility to the need to protect a fixed self-image.
  6. 07:40 Arguments and Wrongness - Being wrong is shown to feel threatening because it stirs identity-protection, especially in conflict, politics, and family life.
  7. 10:05 Many Arms, Many Faces - Avalokiteshvara becomes an image for meeting suffering with many possible responses, and dependent arising is used to question fixed divisions between self and world.
  8. 12:04 Living Point of Practice - Awareness shifts to the immediate psychological conditions where self and world congeal, leading to an invitation to take the other side and soften into relationship.
