Short Talk: Trouble with Samadhi

AudioDharma 15:26 2026-08-26

What if the problem is not that the mind won’t go quiet, but that we have been measuring practice by the wrong sign? The talk starts with a listener’s discouragement about endless inner chatter and turns toward a broader view of samadhi: not just silence, but unification, bodily settling, receptivity, and surrender. It ends by loosening the identity built around “bad meditation,” pointing instead to many possible Dharma doors, ethical life, generosity, and a softer relationship to thought.

Chapters

  1. 00:17 The question of chatter - The talk opens with a listener’s frustration, dread, and hope that meditation can become a different relationship with discursive thinking rather than a fight against it.
  2. 01:48 Practical supports first - Several direct tactics are offered for working with restless attention, including timing, verbal noting, cycling anchors, frequent bells, and listening practices.
  3. 04:18 Not silencing thought - The speaker briefly brings in ACT-style repetition of thoughts and a vivid image about not putting cotton in the ears, to challenge the idea that practice means suppressing thinking.
  4. 05:19 Many Dharma doors - The talk reassures the listener that discursive mind does not exclude them from the path and introduces the idea of many temperaments and many valid entry points.
  5. 07:06 Sila, samadhi, paññā - The threefold training is set out as a framework, with emphasis that wisdom is not simply the result of a quiet mind.
  6. 08:42 What samadhi feels like - Samadhi is expanded beyond graveyard silence to include gathered intention, bodily grounding, tranquility, porosity, and receptivity to the world.
  7. 10:29 Surrender to the moment - The talk argues that samadhi’s function is to make one more available to new information, and that surrender matters more than forcing silence.
  8. 12:41 Ethics and generosity - Ethical conduct, love, and generosity are presented as practical ways of training attention and gathering the mind without making concentration an identity project.
  9. 14:08 Drop the self-story - The ending warns against turning samadhi troubles into selfhood, using a Madhyamaka-style point about ownership and self to dissolve the melodrama of badges and demerits.
