A few words on walking - a continuous yes

AudioDharma 04:05 2026-08-22

The talk asks what it means to move through the world with a continuous yes: to change posture, walk if that is supportive, and meet experience without hiding in self-referential judgment. It points to how a quick, renewed yes can interrupt friction, alienation, arrogance, and self-hate without pretending that everything is pleasant. The emphasis is practical and immediate: see what is left when the habit of no and self-evaluation falls away.

Chapters

  1. 00:00 Change posture - The talk opens with a simple invitation to shift posture and, if helpful, practice walking.
  2. 00:28 A continuous yes - Walking is framed as a kind of continuous yes, a way of meeting the moment without resistance.
  3. 00:56 Not hiding out - The teaching distinguishes this yes from losing track of conventional reality, while naming the tendency to retreat into referential thinking.
  4. 01:36 Friction and alienation - The talk describes how a habitual no can create dualism, persecution, friction, and clinging.
  5. 02:12 Refreshing the yes - The practice is to renew the yes quickly, before identity hardens again into an old self.
  6. 02:29 No room for judgment - With repeated yes, there is less room for self-evaluation, self-hate, arrogance, and the urge to set up a home base.
  7. 03:08 See what remains - The talk invites listeners to notice what is left in the wake of this skillful yes and then explains the retreat timing.
  8. 03:38 Return to movement - The final section gives practical instructions for alternating sitting and walking before closing in silence.
