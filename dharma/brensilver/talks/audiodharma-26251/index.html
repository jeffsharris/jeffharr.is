Short Talk: Bearing Witness to Harm, Moral Responsibility & Hatred

AudioDharma 14:57 2026-09-09

When harm feels so large that resentment seems like the only form of witness, how can care remain honest without turning into hate? The talk works through a listener’s question about outrage, conscience, and moral responsibility, then asks whether suffering always tracks awareness. From there it moves into Buddhist teaching, moral psychology, and the hard work of purifying love so it can stay responsive without becoming revenge.

Chapters

  1. 00:12 The Question of Witnessing - A listener asks how to bear witness to harm without resentment becoming the only reminder that wrongdoing occurred.
  2. 01:59 Taking the Bait - The talk answers playfully, then sets a framework that treats stopping harm as more important than sorting out inner experience alone.
  3. 03:03 Awareness and Suffering - It considers whether suffering always rises with less awareness, and whether pleasure can coexist with deep delusion or lack of conscience.
  4. 04:50 Resentment and Moral Duty - The focus shifts to the feeling that moral responsibility itself demands hatred when one is powerless to stop harm.
  5. 06:10 Non-Hatred as Practice - A classic Buddhist teaching is used to frame the practice of meeting harm with non-hatred while still caring intensely.
  6. 08:23 Disgust Becomes Revenge - Research on moral disgust and a critique of justice-as-vengeance show how aversion can easily harden into punishment and enjoyment of suffering.
  7. 10:22 Refining Love - The talk describes Dharma practice as purifying love so it does not need hatred, nihilism, or cynicism when it cannot control outcomes.
  8. 12:03 Humility and the Larger Order - The ending widens into grief, lineage, and a call to humility before the world, where care continues by joining what already serves life.
