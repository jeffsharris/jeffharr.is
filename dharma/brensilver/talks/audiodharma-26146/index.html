The lure of aversion and the call of love

AudioDharma 26:36 2026-08-22

How do we meet anger without making it an enemy, and how do we keep turning toward love when aversion feels so compelling? The talk moves from a Buddhist warning about hatred, through patience, precepts, and the pull of compulsive habits, toward a practice of repeatedly saying yes to present-moment experience without condoning harm. It ends with a story about embarrassment, falling, and being met gently, as a small example of how unashamedness can open the heart again.

Chapters

  1. 00:00 Love over aversion - The talk opens with a thesis drawn from the Buddha: hatred does not end hatred, and love is the only viable path forward.
  2. 01:34 Anger with sobriety - A striking line from Shantideva is used to show anger’s force without turning the teaching into shame.
  3. 03:29 Non-harming as practice - The talk frames Dharma as ongoing training in non-harming, connecting ethical conduct with a freer heart.
  4. 06:26 A deep yes - Using Suzuki Roshi, the speaker explores saying yes to experience, including anger, without collapsing into self-identification or approval.
  5. 09:21 Respecting symptoms - The talk turns to compassion for our own tangled patterns and the way compulsivity narrows freedom.
  6. 12:02 Ambivalence and dukkha - Thich Nhat Hanh’s image of interbeing helps explain how pleasure and pain, gain and loss, are woven together.
  7. 14:27 Anger as fever - The speaker uses anger’s self-soothing function, then a fever analogy and Ruth King’s distinction between initiatory and transformative anger, to clarify its place.
  8. 18:05 Freedom in expression - A clinical story shows how anger can mark a real step toward freedom when it can be felt and spoken safely.
  9. 21:54 Falling without shame - A story of a fall in the rain becomes a closing image for meeting difficulty with tenderness, humor, and trust, then turning more fully toward love.
