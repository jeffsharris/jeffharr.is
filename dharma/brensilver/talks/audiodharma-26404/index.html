Dharmette: The dharma and romantic love

AudioDharma 21:53 2026-10-07

How does Dharma change romantic love without pretending love can be painless? The talk moves through heartbreak, longing, and the tension between clinging and natural grief, arguing that insight into not-self does not erase desire but can help love become less possessive and more honest. Along the way, it brings in Martha Nussbaum, Rilke, Suzuki Roshi, Octavio Paz, and a scene from Her to explore love as mutual recognition, freedom, and the willingness to let go.

Chapters

  1. 00:19 Love and dukkha - The talk opens with questions about heartbreak, romantic longing, and the line between natural grief and self-created suffering.
  2. 03:52 Need and generosity - Using Martha Nussbaum, the speaker frames love as a realm of need and generosity rather than a simple answer to loneliness.
  3. 07:00 Conditioned beginnings - Rilke, mutual recognition, and karmic compatibility are used to show why love begins conditionally and cannot be reduced to willpower.
  4. 10:14 Saying yes anew - A teaching from Suzuki Roshi and a passage from Ambivalent Zen point toward renewal, commitment, and the limits of renunciation as a solution.
  5. 12:28 Longing without denial - The talk holds together the ending of fantasies of completion with the fact that longing remains and must not be disavowed.
  6. 14:35 Selflessness and possession - The speaker asks what selflessness means in love, then describes how intimacy can blur self-models and make betrayal feel catastrophic.
  7. 17:47 Freedom in love - A long example from Her illustrates the tension between being loved, being possessed, and discovering that love includes the other's freedom.
  8. 20:41 Letting go deeply - The closing image of leaving 'the book' behind gathers the talk into a picture of love that can release without becoming cold.
