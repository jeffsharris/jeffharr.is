Dharmette: A few reflections on pedagogy

AudioDharma 15:18 2026-08-19

What if a Dharma talk is less a finished answer than a way of helping listeners trust their own mind? The talk moves through stories, quotations, and a candid reflection on teaching itself, asking how much of what we learn is shaped by attention, habit, and the hidden autobiography of the teacher. It ends in a quiet claim: confidence and humility can coexist, and the listener’s job is to make meaning and test it honestly.

Chapters

  1. 00:12 Teaching is hard - The talk opens with an honest confession that teaching is difficult and that the best approach is not always clear.
  2. 01:13 Story and mindfulness - A movie-theater image and references to Joseph Goldstein and Martha Nussbaum frame the tension between being inside a story and seeing its constructed nature.
  3. 03:12 Ajahn Chah's warning - A remembered exchange about a 'good talk' becomes a way to unsettle fixed judgments about teaching and listening.
  4. 04:26 Entering the teaching - A quotation from Suzuki Roshi emphasizes not staying at the edge of the teaching, but engaging it fully and honestly.
  5. 05:36 Unclear feedback - The speaker reflects on uncertainty about how practice unfolds, how causes are interpreted, and why feedback about teaching is hard to read.
  6. 07:04 Who stays, who leaves - Selective attrition and missing data are used to show why the people still listening are not a neutral sample.
  7. 08:20 Attention shapes teaching - A story about students conditioning a professor illustrates how teachers can be shaped by audience response, even without noticing it.
  8. 10:14 Information overload - The talk turns to the modern Dharma environment, consumer expectations, and the difference between passive listening and deeper grappling.
  9. 12:18 Humility and confidence - Nietzsche’s idea of philosophy as confession leads to a closing call for epistemic humility, broad wisdom, and trust in one’s own mind.
