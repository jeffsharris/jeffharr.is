3 Characteristics: Impermanence & Uncertainty (Anicca)

AudioDharma 49:08 2026-07-28

What changes when we stop treating experience as something to sort, secure, and control? The talk begins with the pressure of uncertainty and develops into a close look at anicca as both impermanence and instability, showing how insight matures through letting go rather than by solving life. Along the way, the speaker connects retreat structure, anxiety, grief, gratitude, and mortality to the gradual softening of grasping and the possibility of a steadier, less defended way of meeting the present.

Chapters

  1. 01:23 Three characteristics - The talk introduces the three characteristics as a challenge to our habitual drive to organize life into yes and no, pleasant and unpleasant.
  2. 04:55 Insight turns searing - A cited passage on the force of the three characteristics shows how clear seeing can release craving and make grasping feel futile.
  3. 07:00 Insight as process - The speaker describes insight as gestational, shaped by investigation, and requiring kindness as illusion and false ground are grieved.
  4. 13:20 Stability before novelty - A neuroscience reference helps frame retreat as a careful balance between predictability and the openness needed for new perception.
  5. 16:10 Anicca as uncertainty - The talk shifts to uncertainty as a lived force that arouses stress, worry, and the mind's urge to seek information and control.
  6. 20:10 Thinking as defense - The speaker explores how thinking becomes a way to manage uncertainty, how feeling is quickly turned into story, and how hope and fear shape attention.
  7. 25:56 Preferences and fear - A Zen line on having no preferences is used to show that likes and dislikes often arise from grasping and fear rather than simple greed.
  8. 32:14 Present care and impermanence - The talk moves through present-centered practice, dependent origination, grief, and the way love makes impermanence personal and painful.
  9. 40:23 Mortality, gratitude, release - The ending turns toward mortality, gratitude, and late-life reflection, suggesting that some inconsolability remains even as the heart grows more spacious.
