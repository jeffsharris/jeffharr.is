Q & A on metta

AudioDharma 22:34 2026-08-22

How do gratitude, compassion, and a “big yes” stay honest when suffering is real and power has limits? The talk moves from a question about anger and a fever metaphor into a practical map of the Brahma-viharas, where gratitude is tied to mudita and sharpened by remembering dukkha, finitude, and change. It then turns to equanimity, showing how love can remain open without becoming compulsive, intrusive, or falsely promising an outcome.

Chapters

  1. 00:00 Opening and first question - The talk begins with a brief opening and a question about anger, framed through a fever metaphor as something that can be adaptive without being final.
  2. 02:26 Gratitude and mudita - Gratitude is placed within mudita, then explored as something that becomes more poignant when held together with awareness of dukkha, finitude, and change.
  3. 05:32 Compassion with limits - The talk turns to love in the face of suffering, asking how the heart stays open when it cannot fix what hurts and must let go of compulsive effort.
  4. 09:13 What the big yes means - A question about a "big yes" leads into a careful distinction between wholehearted acceptance and rationalizing greed or other habits of delusion.
  5. 14:57 Defensiveness and rehearsal - A new question shifts to defensiveness, rehearsed conversations, and the wish to respond freshly rather than from a protective script.
  6. 18:18 Learning to say no - The response suggests that a clear no may be needed before the heart can truly relax, especially when love has felt costly or careless.
  7. 20:57 Fever falls away - The closing movement returns to the fever image, describing a gradual nursing process in which the old model of self loosens and love becomes safer and clearer.
