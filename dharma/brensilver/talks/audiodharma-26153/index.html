Dharma Talk: Reflections on Love and Kindness

AudioDharma 47:38 2026-08-22

How do love and kindness show up when practice stops feeling abstract and starts loosening the grip of fear, selfing, and the need to win? The talk moves from a reflection on empty, radiant, responsive mind to a careful argument that insight is only trustworthy when it leaves love in its wake. It closes with a personal story of friendship, loss, and a visit to Koyasan, gathering the teaching into the sense that we are living inside a long lineage of care.

Chapters

  1. 00:00 Opening gratitude - The talk begins by honoring volunteers and the conditions that make the gathering possible.
  2. 02:11 Reading each other clearly - A poem frames the need to know one another honestly so that life together does not drift into darkness.
  3. 06:28 Conditioned mind, open experience - The speaker contrasts essentializing language with a Theravada view of mind as conditioned process, then turns to the felt transparency of experience.
  4. 10:35 What remains after letting go - Through references to impermanence and rich vacuity, the talk explores how pain thickens when things feel solid and how practice reveals greater openness.
  5. 17:36 Love as fruit and path - Love is presented as the natural expression of wisdom, and also as what reduces friction so insight can deepen.
  6. 25:27 Courage and the wish to win - The talk examines the courage to change, the circuitous path into kindness, and the way egoic competition blocks love.
  7. 33:18 Selfing grows less compelling - Using the sutta line about ownership and the remark about thinking of oneself, the speaker points to the softening of autobiography and self-pressure.
  8. 39:39 Lineage, loss, and care - A family story in Japan and a rainy visit to Koyasan open into a closing sense of sacred time and a vow to contribute to a lineage of love.
