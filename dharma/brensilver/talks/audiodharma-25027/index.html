Guided Meditation: Refuge

AudioDharma 28:16 2026-03-11

How can refuge be taken not as an idea, but as a way the heart softens in real time? The talk keeps returning to simple phrases—take refuge, call in love or gratitude, let the deep mind relax—while guiding attention into the body, toward reverence, and into a larger frame than one’s immediate concerns. It later widens into reflection on samsara, the stream of Dharma, and Krishnamurti’s warning about the split between subject and object, showing refuge as a way to step out of melodrama and into spaciousness.

Chapters

  1. 00:12 Taking Refuge - The meditation opens by repeating the invitation to take refuge and to let other concerns fall away.
  2. 00:51 Love and Reverence - Refuge is linked to Buddha, Dharma, Sangha, and to calling in love, gratitude, or reverence to soothe the heart amid samsara.
  3. 02:26 Relaxing the Deep Mind - The instruction turns to signaling the deep mind that it is safe to relax and letting attention find a resting place.
  4. 03:57 Body as Home - Attention is guided through the body, the breath, and the sense of density where body meets space, with an emphasis on simplicity.
  5. 05:50 Settling Again - The refuge phrases return in a quieter, more repetitive rhythm, reinforcing ease and letting go.
  6. 08:03 Held by Dharma - After the repeated namaste-like refrain, the talk opens into a broader sense of being held by the Dharma across time and by the stream of wisdom and love.
  7. 15:06 Less Identification - The teaching shifts from personal concern to spaciousness, suggesting that loosening identification makes life less pinched by time.
  8. 22:22 Subject and Object - A brief reference to Krishnamurti frames suffering as the split between subject and object, linking back to the talk’s wider view.
