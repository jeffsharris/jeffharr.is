Guided Meditation: If you know where are you are, you're not here

AudioDharma 29:19 2026-09-02

What happens if practice is less about doing and more about not stirring? The talk unfolds as a guided meditation on letting experience settle, using the Buddha’s invitation to stop what can be stopped and to leave the rest alone. Attention is then gathered around breathing, body, and sound, with the emphasis on relaxing the habit of reiterating oneself and predicting the next moment.

Chapters

  1. 00:00 Settling In - The practice opens with a gentle invitation to arrive, make space, and begin together.
  2. 01:30 Letting Things Settle - The talk uses the image of nectar settling in juice to suggest allowing experience to quiet down on its own.
  3. 02:37 What Can Stop - The speaker turns to the Buddha’s instruction to stop what can be stopped, while letting what cannot stop continue.
  4. 04:15 Not Stirring - The emphasis narrows to the felt question of what it means not to interfere with experience.
  5. 06:50 How Long Is Now - A brief inquiry into the present moment points toward how quickly it passes and how little needs to be added.
  6. 07:50 Gathering Attention - Attention is gathered around breathing, body, and sound as a skillful support for steadiness.
  7. 18:47 Releasing the Future - The talk explores how the mind builds a future self and how practice can ease the agitation of prediction.
